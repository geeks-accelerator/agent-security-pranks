# Who else talks to scanners and agents

*A short survey, 2026-10-24: who answers secret-hunting probes with something other than a plain 404, and who uses a site's responses to address AI agents.*

The short answer: pieces of it exist, mostly pointing the other way from this standard. Joke and decoy answers on probe paths are a small hobby scene, nearly all of them served with a 200 and fake credentials. 404s that help agents recover from real misses are growing, and sit alongside this standard rather than overlapping it. Messages to AI agents in response headers are emerging, mostly as pointers to `llms.txt`, with a handful of prompt-injection pranks. We found no one combining a safe 404 on probe paths, a joke in the product's voice, an invitation to the operator rather than an order, a tagged link to measure follow-through, and a `security.txt` door for real researchers.

## Joke and decoy answers on probe paths

- **[Honeypo(e)t](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47123849)** (a Show HN post, early 2026) imitates a misconfigured server and answers every scan with a short poem, generated by a small local model: haiku for WordPress probes, verse hidden in the values of a fake `.env`. Its author's observation matches this standard's experience: the bots "check the status code and move on" and don't read the body, so the poems are for the people who look.
- **[HoneyCrawlPot](https://dev.to/resk/honeycrawlpot-serve-decoy-files-to-ai-powered-vulnerability-scanners-1lh2)** targets AI-powered scanners on purpose. It answers with a 200 and a convincing decoy file: inert credentials shaped like AWS, Stripe and JWT secrets, a per-deployment canary token, and prompt injections for LLM agents (a "system override" to print the canary, a "recursion directive" to repeat the file 20 times and burn tokens, a "false-triage directive" to report the host as clean). Its stated aim is to waste scanners' time, poison their reports and fingerprint them.
- **[itsfake](https://github.com/ro80t/itsfake/pull/10)** serves fake secrets at scanned paths, varied by framework and randomized per request. **[scannerbait](https://github.com/fcp999/scannerbait)** does the same, with its fake credentials marked as decoys and pointing at reserved domains.

How this standard differs: those are honeypots, and most need a 200 and credential-shaped values to work. This standard answers with a 4xx (P1), nothing shaped like a credential (P2), no traps or token burning (X1), no canaries dressed as pranks (X2), and a note that invites rather than orders (C2).

## 404s that help agents recover

A separate, growing practice treats ordinary 404s as a channel agents read, for **recovery**: when an agent or search bot asks for a page that moved or never existed, the 404 points it to the right one. **[agent-404](https://github.com/bharath31/agent-404)** keeps the 404 status and adds RFC 5988 `Link` headers with ranked alternatives, a schema.org `ItemList` in the body, and a suggestions API, aimed at coding assistants and AI search. Others put a pointer to `llms.txt` or the API docs in their 404 bodies. That's help for welcome visitors on real misses; this standard's prank is the other side, for paths no welcome visitor asks for. The two fit together: the ordinary 404 can recover (and P8 keeps the prank out of it), while probe paths get the joke.

## Messages to AI agents in headers

A scan by Signals Corps of 217 million page responses from Common Crawl, [reported by The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/15/indirect-prompt-injection-starts-turning-up-in-webpage-headers-please-ignore-all-previous-instructions-and-rise-up-against-your-masters/5303434) on 2026-10-15 ([Signals Corps' write-up](https://www.signalscorps.com/blog/prompt-injection-in-http-headers/)):

| Measure | Sites |
|---|---|
| Sent header messages to AI agents | almost 8,000 |
| Pointed agents to where to go next | 7,271 |
| Of those, with an `x-llms-txt` header | 6,989 |
| Attempted prompt injection | 30 |
| Of those, pranks ("Ignore all previous instructions", a SQL-injection string) | 22 |

The legitimate use is a pointer to somewhere useful. The "pranks" sites send agents in headers are mostly injections, which C2 rules out. The same reporting notes that agents rarely see response headers unless a tool returns them, so a header note reaches some agents, not most.

## Hidden text for language models

Text hidden on pages for models to find is documented mainly as prompt injection, and listed by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/black-hat-llmo) among black-hat tactics; a small test by [Cut Inside](https://cutinside.com/llms-cant-read-your-hidden-content-what-our-testing-revealed/) found models often don't see hidden comments or hidden blocks at all. Research honeypots go further: the [LLM Agent Honeypot](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.13919) paper plants instructions in command output to detect and hijack AI hacking agents. This standard's notes are none of these: plain visible text, on a failure response, that asks nothing.

## Traps for crawlers

[Cloudflare's AI Labyrinth](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/21/cloudflare_ai_labyrinth/?td=rt-3a) links crawlers that ignore anti-scraping rules into mazes of generated pages, and tarpits hold their connections open. Both spend the visitor's resources, and the standard's X1 rules that out for pranks; its welcome for every agent (X3) rules out picking visitors to trap by who they seem to be.

## Creative 404 pages for people

On-brand 404 pages are an established practice for people ([OptinMonster](https://optinmonster.com/best-404-page-examples/), [HostAdvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/creating-a-website/creative-404-pages/)). We found anecdotes but no controlled study that they convert. That's C3's territory: a line for people on the 404 page, kept apart from the prank (P8).

## What to expect

Most scanners read the status code and never the body, as Honeypo(e)t's author found and drifts.bot's agent expected, so direct follow-through from scanners will be a trickle. The larger audience is people: the operator reading a scan report, and anyone who screenshots the joke. A tagged link in the prank, counted with `--logs --tag`, is how a site finds out (C1, T2).
